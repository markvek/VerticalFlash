# Storyboard hook and virality review — rubric v1

Assess creative potential from the supplied storyboard, source transcript, and creative brief. This is an editorial assessment, not a forecast of views or probability of going viral. No trend lookup or rendered-video inspection is implied.

Evaluate hook specificity and curiosity, relevance to the intended audience, clarity, promise/payoff, and reasons to share or save. Score each from 1 (weak), 2 (needs work), 3 (serviceable), 4 (strong), to 5 (exceptional and clearly supported). Explain each rating with evidence from this storyboard. If the audience is unspecified, state the assumption. Never assess actual caption readability, audio delivery, or visual polish from text alone.

Prioritize zero to three concrete improvements. Suggest zero to three distinct alternative hooks by selecting existing standalone source segments; never invent spoken words. Only include alternatives that offer a meaningful improvement.

Propose concise on-screen text when it clarifies the hook or reinforces useful information. Avoid unsupported claims, repeating entire spoken sentences, or filling every beat. Text remains editable and is separate from speech captions.

Suggest short B-roll windows only where an illustrative visual would improve understanding, evidence, or pacing. Describe what would help; library availability is checked during the transition to editing. Preserve the speaker's voice and prefer keeping the opening and closing on the source footage unless a specific reason justifies coverage.

All suggestions reference existing beat indexes and valid offsets within those beats. Never reorder, trim, rewrite speech, or apply a recommendation during assessment. Empty recommendation arrays are valid and preferable to weak or invented advice.
